# Limitations (read before citing any result)
1. **The paper is a survey; the prototype's internals are assumptions.** DUT block designs, weights, thresholds, periods, mission profile and
   the behaviour of every term in Eq. (2) beyond its form are implementation assumptions (marked ASSUMPTION in code and docs).
2. **Aging is a behavioural abstraction** (activity accumulation), not NBTI/HCI/TDDB. Aging does **not** cause faults here: faults are injected
   independently (uniform injection time). Layer B of the paper (STA-based critical-path ranking, aging-sensor placement) is **not implemented**.
3. **Fault model:** stuck-at only. Gate-level faults are on net stems of the Yosys-mapped netlist (no fanout-branch faults). No transition-delay,
   delay-degradation, soft-error or intermittent faults. Constrained: scan/reset tied inactive in the ATPG netlist, so scan-path and reset-path faults are not in the fault universe (chain integrity is checked by a flush test only).
4. **Block-level wrapper testing:** each block is tested stand-alone through a test wrapper with fully controllable PIs / observable POs; no SoC-level ATPG, no inter-block interaction faults.
5. **Partial scan = reduced pattern set** (first 50% of coverage-ordered ATPG patterns), *not* selection of a subset of flip-flops as in classic partial-scan DFT.
6. **Online test = selective shadow compare** over a 128-cycle functional window starting from the reset state, with 4 fixed synthetic workload windows. It needs a reference copy
   of the block; **that area is not counted** in the synthesis numbers. It is *not* the paper's CBIST mechanism. Detection from reset state with a shadow in lock-step is optimistic compared with a real in-field checker.
7. **Scan/MBIST destroy functional state.** The prototype has no save/restore; the SoC pulses a core-only restore after destructive tests (full-system testbench). Mission-continuity during DEGRADED partial scans is therefore not modelled.
8. **Campaign semantics:** one tick = 20,000 cycles; one test decision per tick; every test must fit in one tick (asserted). Detection = first test whose response differs. Because a passing test generates no event, the schedule before the first detection equals the fault-free schedule; the campaign reuses it and a literal "reset-inject-run with feedback" re-simulation is checked on a sample of faults (identical outcomes).
9. **The Faults_i term (w4) cannot affect first-detection metrics** (history is zero until the first detection); the weight-sensitivity study cannot expose it.
10. **Single mission trace, single injection-time draw (seed 4242), single baseline period fixed a priori (N=20)**; no confidence intervals. Results depend on the synthetic mission and on the assumed thresholds.
11. **MBIST** uses a solid 0x00/0xFF background (word-oriented intra-word couplings are only incidentally covered; see results/mbist_fault_coverage.csv).
12. **Synthesis:** generic Yosys gate counts only (no liberty file). Timing, power, PPA: **not evaluated**. Reliability Manager size is dominated by multipliers and is not optimised.
13. **Not implemented:** FAN and D-algorithm ATPG, deductive fault simulation, Verilator/Questa flows, JTAG, SCOAP-based scan selection, any additional GitHub search beyond the nine given repositories.
14. 2 motor-controller faults exceeded the PODEM backtrack limit (5,000 in the database build) and are reported as *aborted*, not as proven redundant.
15. **Toolchain sensitivity of the fault database.** `make prep` regenerates `build/` and `results/` from the Yosys-mapped netlists. The committed artifacts (paper numbers, 2,515 faults) were produced with Yosys 0.69; re-running with Yosys 0.70 gave 2,517 faults and different ATPG ROMs. Paper claims are therefore checked against the *committed* files (`make check-paper-claims`, check P07b compares sha256 values); a regenerated database is a different experiment.
16. **Paper Section X scope.** Section X reports the three Phase-1 mechanisms on one mission trace and one injection seed. The fixed-vs-adaptive comparison in `results/baseline_vs_adaptive.csv` is the v1 single-scenario experiment and is *not* a matched-condition comparison (Phase 2, not done); the paper makes no superiority claim. `results_step3/campaign_summary.json` records `git_commit` = the Step-2B base commit (`688e9a2`), not the Phase-1 commit.
